## Introduction
When small businesses and mid‑size firms need urgent help, on‑site IT support can be the lifeline that keeps operations running. Yet, pricing these services is tricky: you must balance your overhead, skill level, and market demand while staying competitive. This guide breaks down the factors that shape a fair on‑site rate, offers real‑world examples, and gives you practical tools to set a price that satisfies both you and your clients.

## 1. Understand the Core Cost Drivers
### 1.1 Labor Hours
The most obvious driver is the number of hours you spend. A complex server migration will cost more than a quick password reset. Estimate the average time per job and multiply by your hourly cost.
### 1.2 Skill Level & Certification
A junior technician can often handle routine tasks for $50‑$70 per hour, while a senior engineer with specialized certifications (e.g., Microsoft Certified Systems Engineer, Cisco CCNA) can command $100‑$150 per hour.
### 1.3 Travel & Logistics
On‑site visits incur travel time, mileage, lodging, and meals. A common rule of thumb is to add 10‑15% of the labor cost for travel expenses.
### 1.4 Tooling & Software Licenses
If you need to bring proprietary diagnostic tools or pay for a temporary software license, factor those costs into the final price.

## 2. Hourly vs. Flat‑Rate Pricing
### 2.1 Hourly Model
The hourly model is transparent: clients pay exactly for the time you spend. It works well for unpredictable jobs where the scope isn’t clear upfront.
**Pros**: Flexibility, easy to justify. **Cons**: Clients may fear runaway bills.

### 2.2 Flat‑Rate Model
A flat rate caps the price for a defined scope. For example, “On‑site network reboot” could be $350 flat, regardless of how many hours it takes.
**Pros**: Predictable for clients, easier marketing. **Cons**: Risk if the job overruns.

Many firms use a hybrid approach: a base flat fee plus an hourly add‑on for overtime.

## 3. Regional Market Variations
Prices vary widely by geography. In tech hubs like San Francisco or New York, hourly rates can reach $200+. In rural areas, rates may drop to $50‑$80 per hour. Use local competitor data and adjust for your cost of living.

## 4. Practical Example Scenarios
### 4.1 Scenario A: Quick Hardware Replacement
- **Scope**: Replace a faulty laptop battery.
- **Estimated Time**: 1 hour.
- **Hourly Rate**: $75.
- **Travel**: 0.5 hour at $30.
- **Total**: $75 + $30 = $105. A flat fee of $110 is reasonable.

### 4.2 Scenario B: Server Rebuild
- **Scope**: Reinstall OS, restore from backup, and test services.
- **Estimated Time**: 8 hours.
- **Hourly Rate**: $120.
- **Travel & Lodging**: 2 hours + $200 hotel.
- **Tools**: $50 for backup software.
- **Total**: (8×$120)+(2×$30)+$200+$50 = $1,170. Offer a flat $1,200 to simplify.

### 4.3 Scenario C: Network Security Audit
- **Scope**: Scan, patch, and configure firewall.
- **Estimated Time**: 5 hours.
- **Hourly Rate**: $110.
- **Travel**: 1 hour.
- **Total**: $550 + $30 = $580. A flat $600 covers unexpected delays.

## 5. Bundling Services for Value
Clients love packages that bundle routine maintenance with on‑site visits. For example:
- **Monthly Maintenance Package**: 2 on‑site visits + remote monitoring = $1,200/month.
- **Quarterly Security Suite**: On‑site audit + 24/7 support = $3,500/quarter.

Bundling reduces the perceived cost per service and locks in recurring revenue.

## 6. Negotiating and Protecting Your Bottom Line
- **Set Clear Scope**: Document what is included and what is extra.
- **Use Time‑Tracking**: Keep an accurate log to defend overtime charges.
- **Offer a Cap**: Agree on a maximum bill for large jobs to reassure clients.
- **Ask for Advance Payment**: For high‑cost jobs, request 30% upfront.
- **Build a Pricing Matrix**: Publish a table of common services with suggested rates; transparency builds trust.
